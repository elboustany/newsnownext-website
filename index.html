Today’s Brief

Tuesday 15 September 2026

Daily brief — Tuesday, Sept. 15, 2026 The Federal Reserve begins its two-day meeting today. The decision is due Wednesday at 2 p.m. Eastern. Markets price about a 90 percent chance of a quarter-point hike, to a 3.75–4.00 percent range — the first increase under Chair Kevin Warsh and the first since 2023. The data path that got here is clear. August payrolls rose 162,000, well above forecasts. Unemployment stayed at 4.1 percent. Headline CPI rose 0.4 percent and 3.4 percent year over year. Core CPI rose 0.3 percent on the month, hotter than expected. Producer prices were up 0.4 percent and 5.4 percent over the year. Energy is the main driver. Diesel has traded above $6 a gallon. Brent is above $100. The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5 percent Monday, its highest since late 2023. The 30-year is near 5.35 percent. Warsh has said inflation must move toward 2 percent “clearly and at sufficient speed.” Governor Christopher Waller had been willing to hold if August inflation cooled. It did not. President Trump has pressed the Fed to cut, not hike, weeks before midterms. A hold with hike odds this high would likely jolt the long end of the bond market. Stocks slipped Monday. Nvidia and other chipmakers fell after OpenAI and Anthropic executives called for a slower pace of AI development. CrowdStrike jumped after results. Oracle had already lifted on a $26 billion jump in backlog. Copart agreed to buy ACV Auctions for about $1.9 billion. This week also brings retail sales, industrial production, housing starts and the Philly Fed and Empire surveys. The Bank of England meets Thursday. The press conference after Wednesday’s vote will matter as much as the hike itself: whether Warsh signals one move or a new cycle.
